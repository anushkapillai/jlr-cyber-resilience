How to use it. Pick a starting scenario or move the sliders on the left. The results on the right update straight away. Every cost, chance and time saving is an illustrative placeholder, not a JLR figure, so try your own.

How to read it. “Net expected benefit” is the loss an option is expected to avoid over three years, minus what it costs. Below zero means it costs more than it is expected to save.

How big is one attack?

What each option means and how the model works
  • A. Prevention first spends on stopping attackers from getting in. It cuts the chance of an attack by 30% and saves a quarter of the recovery weeks.
  • B. Recovery first assumes attackers will get in and spends on restoring systems quickly. It does not change the chance of an attack but saves the weeks you choose.
  • C. Insurance and financing pays premiums and arranges credit so an insurer covers part of the loss, up to £150m.
  • D. Recovery plus extras is option B plus some targeted prevention (10% fewer attacks) and an insurance policy with a £100m limit.
  • Event loss equals weeks of output lost, times weekly sales of about £508m, times the profit lost per £1 of sales, plus a £260m one-off cost. The one-off cost is the figure in JLR’s Annual Report 2025/26.
  • Expected loss equals the chance of the attack times the loss if it happens, plus the chance and loss of a non-cyber outage. Nothing is discounted over the three years.
